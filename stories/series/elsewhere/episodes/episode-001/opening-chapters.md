# Elsewhere — Episode 1: The Photograph
## Opening chapters (reconstructed draft)

### Prologue — The Photograph

Five years earlier, the rain had been falling for three days.

At 2:17 in the morning, Hex sat in a black car outside a railway station, watching water slide down the windscreen. He was thirty-two, tired, and accustomed to being sent places without knowing why.

His phone lit up.

PLATFORM NINE. LOCKER FORTY-THREE. SIX MINUTES.

“Six minutes,” he muttered. “How generous.”

He stepped into the rain and crossed the nearly empty concourse. The station smelled of wet concrete, old coffee, and electricity. Locker forty-three opened with a code sent to his phone. Inside was a brown envelope, unmarked except for black tape.

He took it into an empty waiting room and broke the seal.

OPERATION GLASSHOUSE.

Intercept an encrypted archive taken by a courier named Elise Varen. Recover the archive. Do not engage civilian contacts.

Six photographs were enclosed. The last showed a woman outside a café: pink-purple hair, dark clothes, a septum ring, and an expression halfway between amusement and defiance.

Hex looked at that picture longer than he intended.

CIVILIAN REFERENCE N-17. NON-OPERATIONAL. DIRECT CONTACT PROHIBITED.

Her name was Ninja.

“And what are you doing in this mess?” he murmured.

His phone rang. Mara, his handler.

“You have the package?”

“Unfortunately. Why is a civilian in my operational file?”

“She’s a reference point. Nothing more.”

“That's a strange answer.”

“It’s the only one you need. Recover the archive, Hex. No improvising.”

“You say that like it’s a personality defect.”

“On you, it is.”

The call ended.

Hex returned the photograph to its envelope. He had no idea that this woman he had never met would become the one face he could not forget.

### Chapter One — The Night Everything Went Wrong

The industrial compound stood beyond the city, where roads dissolved into loading bays and abandoned warehouses.

Hex arrived before dawn. His assignment was supposed to be simple: find Elise Varen, intercept the archive, leave before anyone noticed.

Simple was a word the Directorate used when it didn't want questions.

From the service road, Hex studied the guards. They weren't positioned to keep intruders out. They were positioned to keep people inside.

“Mara,” he said into his earpiece. “Your security layout doesn't match the briefing.”

“Proceed.”

“There are armed teams at both exits.”

“Containment.”

“Containment of what?”

“Recover the archive.”

Then the first shot rang out.

Hex moved toward the building instead of away. Inside, Elise Varen stumbled through a corridor, clutching a case against her chest. She saw him, stopped, and seemed to recognise what his presence meant.

“You're too late,” she said.

A second shot struck the wall. Hex pulled her behind a steel support.

“Who are those men?”

Elise looked at him as though he had asked whether water was wet. “Yours.”

The fire started moments later. An explosion shook the windows. Hex heard voices beyond a locked steel door and fought the handle until the metal burned his palms.

“Mara! There are civilians trapped in here!”

“Stand down. The archive is the priority.”

“People are dying.”

“I gave you an order.”

He stopped answering her.

He got two people out through a loading entrance. When he turned back, flames had swallowed the corridor. Elise was dead. Others were missing. Six people would ultimately be confirmed killed.

Hex stood outside in the cold dawn, coughing smoke, and understood that the operation had never been an interception. It had been a cleanup.

His earpiece crackled.

“Hex. Return for debrief.”

He looked toward the burning warehouse.

“No.”

A pause.

“Do not make this worse.”

“You already did.”

He pulled the earpiece out and crushed it under his boot.

Within hours, the Directorate classified him as compromised and authorised his recovery. He knew what that word meant.

Hex disappeared with the envelope still in his coat.

### Chapter Two — The Woman Who Didn't Know

Ninja had never heard of Operation Glasshouse.

She knew only that a woman named Elise Varen, who had once helped straighten out a mess involving employment records, had died in a warehouse fire.

At first, the tragedy seemed distant and inexplicable. Then small things began going wrong.

An account was frozen. A document disappeared from a system where it had existed the week before. A complaint was filed using information that was wrong in ways that were difficult to disprove. People who had once been helpful became cautious and distant.

She spent hours on calls, explaining the same facts to strangers who promised to call back and didn't.

She worked in healthcare. She knew how to stay calm when someone else needed her. She knew how to make decisions when things were difficult. But competence didn't make the paperwork disappear, and being resilient didn't make the nights less lonely.

Her apartment became a place of unfinished tasks, cold cups of coffee, dark clothes, and music through her black Sony headphones.

One evening she sat at the kitchen table with a stack of letters, a pen, and the exhausted determination of someone who had run out of patience long before she ran out of problems.

“Right,” she told the paperwork. “Which one of you is ruining my life today?”

No one answered. That was becoming a theme.

She didn't know that her legitimate records had been copied and used to conceal a financial trail connected to the archive Elise carried. When the Directorate erased that trail, it erased pieces of Ninja's life along with it.

She didn't know that a man named Hex had tried to stop the disaster.

And Hex didn't know, yet, how much damage had followed the fire into the life of the woman in the photograph.

They were strangers living with consequences of the same night.

For years, neither would know how close their stories had already come to crossing.
