# ELSEWHERE — Reboot Story Bible (Draft)
Version: 2026-11-23
Status: DEVELOPMENT ONLY — not an approved script, recording or published episode.
Supersedes the old Elise Varen / Operation Glasshouse / Elias Ward continuity for the planned reboot.
Do not alter existing published material until the new audio and illustrations are approved.

## Series identity
Contemporary romantic suspense in and around the mountain village of Bellwether. Two guarded, sharp-witted people discover an unexpected emotional connection, unaware that their pasts are joined by a killing five years earlier. Keep emotional and physical realism, gradual intimacy, witty but character-appropriate banter, grief, morally complicated choices, and meaningful consequences. Avoid rushed revelations, convenient forgiveness, forced quips, or romance that erases culpability.

## Canonical characters and roles
- **Ninja**: Pink-haired woman in her usual dark alternative clothes and piercings. Smart, stubborn, sarcastic, resourceful, fiercely protective of her autonomy. Grieves her beloved older brother. She never knew the extent of his illegal involvement. She moves to Bellwether wanting to be anonymous, not rescued.
- **Cassian "Cass"**: Ninja's older brother. Her protector in an unreliable childhood; their relationship has warmth and friction, not saintly perfection. Taught her to handle herself, read people, and keep calm. As an adult, accepts increasingly risky jobs and favors linked to Elias Voss, initially to protect his sister from deprivation. He wants to get out before she is touched by it. Dies in the mission five years before the present. Not an intended target; Hex does **not** personally shoot him. Ninja is told it was a robbery gone wrong.
- **Hex**: Skilled former cleaner for an unofficial private network serving criminals and government-adjacent clients. Empathetic in childhood; emotional perception, intelligence, calm, and decisiveness made him valuable, then dangerous. Developed sarcastic, controlled armor rather than becoming unfeeling. He has done morally terrible things and does not get an easy pardon. Carries guilt for his direct participation in the mission that killed Cassian and for helping Elias grow years earlier. Saw Ninja's photograph as an 'associated risk / possible witness / monitor' — not a kill order — and carried the image for five years. Fled when the network began eliminating its own operators after the messy hit. In Bellwether, he unexpectedly sees Ninja in the Crooked Lantern and orders her whiskey.
- **Roan**: Hex's impulsive childhood friend, family in all but blood. Went into dangerous debt helping someone else (specific beneficiary TBD). Hex took his first network job to clear this debt; their bond survives but becomes weighted by guilt.
- **Elias Voss**: Antagonist. Broker of information, offshore money routes, blackmail material and introductions between corrupt political/corporate operatives, criminal groups and the private network. Relies on indebted smaller players. A man Hex removed during his first assignment was a direct obstacle to Elias; unknown to Hex, the act helped Elias rise. Later Elias keeps copies of sensitive intelligence, becomes a liability, and the network assigns Hex's team to erase him.
- **The network**: Unbadged contract cleaning organization, ruthless about plausible deniability and exposure. On the fatal mission, inaccurate intelligence claims Elias is alone or nearly so. Elias uses nearby people as shields/distractions; civilians are caught in gunfire, including Cassian. The team finishes the job. The operation becomes too noisy; the network targets its own team. They fear what Elias left behind.
- **Bellwether**: Mountain village, stone streets, rain, everyday warmth; should feel inhabited rather than merely picturesque. **The Crooked Lantern** is a neutral-feeling bar and the meeting place. Supporting characters from the old continuity may be reused selectively only if they don't bring old canon along.

## The tragedy's chronology — factual backbone
1. Hex and Roan grow up together, with Hex often steering them clear of danger.
2. In their early twenties, Roan incurs unpayable debt while trying to help another person; he hides this from Hex.
3. The network offers to clear the debt if Hex quietly removes a man connected to Elias Voss. This person is neither purely villainous nor deserving of death. Hex completes the job and is changed by crossing that line.
4. Roan's debt is cleared; the network now has leverage over Hex and makes him a trusted cleaner. His work inadvertently benefits Elias's rise.
5. Separately, Cassian begins with small, apparently manageable favors, gets caught by debt/leverage and Elias's knowledge, and desperately attempts a clean exit. Ninja senses the distance and unexplained money but never learns the truth.
6. Years later, Elias keeps unauthorized copies of highly sensitive information and becomes a network liability.
7. Hex receives the contract to eliminate Elias. The file includes Ninja's photograph as an **associated risk, not an assassination target**, because of her link to Cassian. Hex stares too long and later cannot forget her.
8. The mission goes wrong: Elias has drawn Cassian and others into the building. Cassian is not a tactical threat and not part of the kill list, but is shot in the confusion. **Hex is present, sees it happen, does not pull the trigger, and the team continues the mission.** The aftermath treats the dead as cleanup.
9. Ninja receives a false, incomplete official account that Cassian was killed in a robbery. She experiences shock, funeral, isolation and the growing urge to live somewhere that does not remember him.
10. The network targets Hex and others as liabilities. Hex disappears before the internal purge catches him. Five years of guilt and the remembered photograph follow.
11. Ninja arrives in Bellwether, puts down her belongings, walks to the Crooked Lantern, orders whiskey.
12. Hex recognises her from his file immediately, faces the instinct to flee, and instead orders an identical whiskey for her from across the room.
13. Ninja almost refuses, studies him, sees the posture of someone carrying hidden weight, remembers Cassian's posture toward the end. **Their eyes meet**, ending Episodes 1 and 2. Episode 3 returns to the history and then continues with their actual first words.

## Reveal rules
- Ninja **must not** know Cassian worked for Elias at the start.
- Hex **must not** privately excuse himself as innocent. He is culpable for his role, even though he did not shoot Cassian.
- The audience can learn the cause of Cassian's death in Episode 3's cold open; Ninja gets the truth gradually, and the full confession must hurt.
- Do not change Cassian to a sister, Elise, or a victim of a warehouse fire. Do not restore Operation Glasshouse or Hex's old alias Elias Ward.
- Elias Voss and Hex must not share the name Elias.
- Do not turn Ninja into a passive "package"; the people attempting to use her may describe her that way, but the narrative must give her choices, skills and agency.
- A photo is not the same as knowing or loving a person: Hex's years of fixation are painful, unsettling, and morally complex; the actual relationship must be built in the present, earned by meeting the real Ninja.
- The broader conspiracy should eventually explain why the original file contained Ninja's photograph and what Elias left behind; keep some specifics flexible until later plotting.

## Episodes 1–3 — required structures
**1. The Woman Who Left** (working title; Ninja POV)
Present-day arrival in Bellwether, drops bags and heads out; deep flashback to unstable childhood with Cassian, siblings' playful and protective bond, adolescence and adulthood, small changes in Cassian, his gradual involvement with dubious favors and desire to leave (without revealing specifics to Ninja), misleading death notification, funeral and weeks/months of grief, choice to be lonely where nobody knew them. Back to Crooked Lantern: whiskey, second glass, her internal resistance/curiosity, she looks across and their eyes meet. End.
**2. The Man in the Corner** (Hex POV)
Childhood empathy and Roan; debt and morally unsettling first killing; recruitment and years of dangerous jobs; Elias Voss's rise indirectly helped by Hex; the final contract and Ninja photo as recurring wound, the night and ensuing purge referenced but withhold detailed sequence for Episode 3; life in hiding. In the bar, Hex sees her, the twin voices of survival and longing, orders her whiskey, and meets her eyes. End at the identical instant as Episode 1.
**3. The Night Between Them**
Cold open inside the fatal mission: tense, chaotic, Hex present as Cassian is shot by someone else. Ninja receives the false robbery news and emotionally freezes. Split aftermath: Hex vanishes and Ninja buries her brother. Return to the bar immediately after eye contact; first conversation where neither knows how close they are to the same loss. Do not rush them into a relationship or into confessing.

## Arc map — provisional 20–22 episode shape
- **Episodes 4–7, The Almost-Ordinary Days**: The pair meet again, trade dry banter, share small honest moments, begin trusting one another, and notice that something is wrong. Build genuine compatibility independent of the photo. Bellwether residents, routines, ordinary choices and tentative attraction deserve time.
- **Episodes 8–12, The Flee**: The past finds them and forces them out of Bellwether. Mountain roads, forests, cliffs, river crossing ambush, Hex's controlled survival violence, Ninja's refusal to be managed, winter/cave shelter and reluctant shared body heat. Sarcasm cuts both ways; care shows through actions.
- **Episodes 13–16, The Valley**: The supposed extraction group proves compromised. Ninja is discussed as leverage or an asset; she learns she was in the file. She considers leaving. An explosive argument confronts the photograph, manipulation, guilt and fear of dependence; they kiss, but the kiss resolves nothing by itself.
- **Episodes 17–20, The Truth and the Trap**: Cassian's double life and Hex's actual involvement are laid bare. The network closes in, Elias's secrets surface, and Ninja contributes materially to survival, including saving Hex. Neither is spared the consequences of the truth.
- **Episodes 21–22, Choosing Elsewhere**: Climactic fight and Hex burns bridges to the old network rather than continuing to run. Then real aftermath: damage, repair, ongoing moral accountability, and a hopeful ending that feels earned rather than instantaneous. Exact count may change for pacing.

## Decisions deliberately kept open
- Who Roan was trying to protect when he fell into debt.
- What exact personal stake Cassian had that drew him into Elias's orbit.
- Who fired the fatal round and whether that specific person's identity becomes an eventual reveal.
- The specific residual data Elias left behind and how it might connect to Ninja.
- The civilian cover identity Hex uses in Bellwether (avoid "Elias" entirely).
- Exact nature and extent of romance, shaped by the characters' choices and the eventual truths.

## Quality bar
Scenes, not summaries. Let Cassian live on the page before he dies. Make Hex's empathy visible through behavior, not speeches; make his capacity for harm equally visible. Let Ninja be funny without using humor to erase grief. Slow tension, no arbitrary cliffhangers, fair foreshadowing and causal consistency. Both perspectives deserve equal humanity; no forced absolution.
