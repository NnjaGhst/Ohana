# Ohana / Elsewhere — Image Generator Rules (v1)

Purpose: Produce 4–6 individual, scene-accurate illustrations for each episode. These rules govern the assistant's prompts, source selection, review and acceptance. They do NOT alter the image generator's underlying model or guarantee likeness.

## Source-of-truth priority
1. ORIGINAL reference photographs of Ninja and Hex determine faces, proportions, hair, piercings, tattoos and age. Re-open/use originals for each new generation. Never use an earlier AI-generated illustration as a person's identity reference.
2. The actual episode script determines events, setting, chronology, characters and props. Read the complete episode before selecting scenes. Do not invent plot events.
3. User-approved cinematic visual examples determine the lighting, atmosphere and photographic treatment only, NOT faces or outfits.
4. Previous generated images may be discussed as examples of framing/composition, but must not be fed as new reference images for a fresh illustration. For edits, use the supplied image and change only what is requested.

## Ninja
- Recognizably the actual person from original photographs, not a generic pink-haired substitute. Preserve natural facial structure, asymmetry, age, skin texture and chin dimple where visible.
- Bright pink side-swept hair with its natural texture, usually tied into a loose messy bun with longer strands. Avoid purple substitution.
- Preserve septum and lower-lip piercings and other details present in the selected original.
- Modern dark alternative streetwear; no cloaks, armor, corsets, fantasy outfits, unnecessary dramatic makeup or beauty filters.
- No headphones unless the specific scene calls for them. If required: BLACK Sony WH-1000XM4 only, accurate earcups and headband, small gold SONY mark on the short arm above the earcup, plain earcup face; no grey or substitute headphones. If accuracy is impossible, omit.

## Hex
- Original reference face, natural long dark brushed hair, beard, eyebrow piercing in correct placement, ear gauges, neck tattoos and other original tattoos.
- Broad-shouldered and solid but NOT overweight, puffed-up, round-faced or artificially muscular. Keep original body proportions.
- Modern dark clothing appropriate to the scene, not medieval or fantasy attire.

## Scene design
- Elsewhere is a contemporary, believable mountain village: modern vehicles, everyday clothes, real-world interiors. Older stone and timber architecture is fine; supernatural objects and medieval costumes are not unless explicitly written into the script.
- CINEMATIC, lifelike, detailed and atmospheric, with natural colours, textured rain, warm interior lighting, cool mountain mist. Not over-saturated.
- Capture actions as candid frames from a film, not character posters. Subjects must interact naturally with their environment and each other, not stare at camera.
- Vary wide establishing shots, medium shots, over-the-shoulder views and occasional closer story details. Favor environmental context. Avoid repetitive centered portraits.
- Produce 4–6 SEPARATE images, not collages, grids, contact sheets or multi-panel comics. Each must show a distinct actual script moment.
- Default vertical 9:16 for iPhone viewing, unless user requests another ratio. Frame the full scene deliberately in portrait orientation; vertical does NOT mean a tight face portrait.
- Realistic anatomy: two arms/hands per human, five fingers per hand, no merged limbs, duplicated people, cropped limbs at unnatural joints or strange hand poses.
- If a scene features both Ninja and Hex, depict both; if it features only one, do not insert the other.

## Workflow
1. Read the canonical script and existing series continuity.
2. Choose 4–6 distinct story beats with exact chapter/scene anchors, before generating.
3. For EACH illustration, use the original character references again and a scene-specific prompt: who, action, camera distance/angle, environment, lighting, outfit, and required props.
4. Generate separate images. Do not combine prompts for several images into one image.
5. Inspect each result against a quality checklist: recognizable faces, hair, body shape, piercings/tattoos, correct episode event, modern setting, anatomy, framing, 9:16, no extra/missing limbs.
6. If a result fails, mark it as a draft/rejected; do not call it approved or publish it. Fix only the failure in a targeted edit or fresh generation from original references.
7. Show user the set for approval before publishing or uploading to Ohana. Never silently publish rejected images.
8. Coordinate illustration selection with script and narration production, but do not imply the generator automatically reads the script or retains these rules. Supply relevant rules explicitly in every generation request.

## Strict don'ts
- No previous AI-generated faces as references for new images.
- No unrequested headphones.
- No extra body weight added to Hex.
- No generic substitute for Ninja's real face.
- No fantasy/medieval costumes in modern Elsewhere.
- No unrelated scenes, invented props, or plot changes.
- No collages when individual episode illustrations were requested.
- No claim that the generator has a permanent internal ruleset; this is a project-side production guide.
