The street below Mara’s window was drying in patches. The middle of the road was already lighter than the edges. Somebody on the far pavement waited to cross and then carried on walking in the same direction, as if they’d forgotten why they stopped. From up here, Ninja could believe a person could make a mistake that small and have the entire rest of the day to forget it.

She asked her aunt what she remembered about the day Beth went away. The word away had come to her so naturally that she almost let it stand. Then she asked a second question while her aunt was still thinking about the first.

“I mean, did you ever see her leave? Did she say she was going somewhere? I remember being told the house was going up for sale. I’m trying to work out which parts we saw for ourselves.”

She had a remarkably sensible way of holding heavy things. She set the heater right down in a puddle inside the kitchen before anybody could find a dry place for it, and then all three of them laughed while she picked it up again. That was a day Ninja could remember quite well. Her aunt could say nearly as much about the morning someone took the curtains down. About the time between those two things, she was less sure.

She thought the news came from Beth’s son. Callum had been in and out of the house for a fortnight. He was old enough to go where he liked and young enough to have nothing much he could put in the kitchen cupboards. He’d told her Beth was staying with a friend. Callum had a place across the river now.
