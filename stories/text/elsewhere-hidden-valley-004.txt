# Elsewhere: The Hidden Valley
## Episode 4 — The Name at the Door

Previously on Elsewhere, Ninja came to Bellwether for three quiet nights and met a mechanic who called himself Elias. They shared whiskey, made an unreasonable number of jokes about a marzipan rabbit, and ended up in room six at the hotel. Elias knew Ninja's name before she introduced herself. He had seen it written under a photograph five years earlier, on the night Elise died.

Someone knocked three times. Ninja opened one eye and put a hand out for the water on the little table. A grey line of daylight ran between the curtains. She had to look at the chair with both their coats on it before she remembered where she was.

Elias was already sitting up. She watched him for a second, expecting the man who had laughed when she got one boot stuck the previous night. He was listening with his head turned slightly. The bed had hardly moved.

A man on the landing spoke through the door. “I've got something for Elise.”

Ninja put the water down. A little of it ran across the top of the table. She saw Elias flinch at the name straight away, as if the man outside had tried the handle.

“Who is it?”

There was a pause. “Daniel. I worked with your sister.”

His voice was older than she'd expected, and a little out of breath. She got up and found her trousers. Elias picked up the wooden chair by the window and moved it aside, clearing the short walk to the door. Then he went and stood far enough back that she could pass him. She was glad he had done that. There was plenty to be angry about already, and she hadn't yet worked out where to begin.

The man on the landing wore a light coat with rain on its shoulders. He looked at her and took off a flat cap. There was a faint dent on the bridge of his nose from a pair of glasses he wasn't wearing.

“You went to the office,” he said. “Five years ago. Over the appliance shop.”

Ninja looked at his coat and then at his hands. The right thumb had a dark mark beside the nail. She remembered seeing that thumb pressed to the brake lever of a bicycle in her stairwell.

“You brought her bike back.”

He nodded. “I said you could come for her things when it was quiet. They emptied the place that week. I've thought a lot about what I'd say if I saw you again.”

“How did you know I was here?”

“I came to see somebody from the old office. He saw you last night. When he said you'd gone to the hotel, I thought I'd have until breakfast.”

At that, he looked past her and saw Elias. It took him a moment. Then the expression on his face became so ordinary and weary that Ninja knew neither of them had mistaken the other for anyone else.

“Hex,” Daniel said. “Does she know?”

Elias put a hand on the back of the chair. “She knows the name I use here.”

Ninja turned to him. There were a few details she'd never be able to recover about that instant. She did remember the mark in the carpet from where the chair had been. She remembered that neither man moved to fill the silence.

“And how did you know Elise?”

He swallowed. “We worked together. I was meant to pick something up from her the night she died.”

Ninja could hear the little radiator ticking. It made the same noise as the one in her flat whenever the heating had been off for a while. She walked over and picked up her glass because it was still there and it was something she knew how to do. Then she asked Daniel to come inside and close the door.

Daniel set the flat cap on his knee. He sat at the end of the bed for less than a minute before getting up and sitting in the chair instead. The room was too small for three people to have much room to think. Ninja took her coat off the back of the chair and put it on, though she wasn't cold.

Daniel said Elise had given him a copy of some figures from the office. She'd asked him to look up a payment that went through twice, because one of the two men named on it said he'd only been given money once. It had taken him a few days. By the time he had an answer, Elise had stopped coming into work.

“Did anyone ask you about that after she died?”

“A man at the office did. He asked if she'd left any receipts behind. I told him she used her notebook when she wanted to remember something.”

Hex spoke from the other side of the room. “Did they know about the copy?”

“I spent five years hoping they didn't.”

He took a worn shirt envelope from inside his coat. Ninja reached for it, but Daniel kept one finger on the flap.

“This is a letter she left with the copy. I read the first line. I was trying to work out who had to have it. The rest is yours.”

The envelope had a corner folded in on itself. It was the kind of thing Elise might have picked up from a counter and saved because it had an unused side. Ninja let the thought settle before she pulled it toward her.

She could see the date in Elise's handwriting. There was a little diagonal line under the final number. Elise always did that when she wanted someone to understand that a date was important. Two days before she died.

Hex took half a step backward. Ninja noticed that he moved his foot almost carefully, as if she had told him to give her some space and he was working out how much. She took the letter to the window, where the light was better.

Elise had written that she was sorry Ninja had been doing so much on her own lately. She should have asked about it the last time she came round. They could talk on Sunday if she was home. Then came a line she might have added later. If a man named Carver ever turned up at the flat, Ninja was to leave him on the landing and phone her.

Ninja read it through twice. The first time she went all the way to the end. The second time she stopped at Sunday. She'd kept a place free that Sunday without knowing what for. Most of the day she'd sat in an armchair at her aunt's while people carried cups in and out.

“Did Carver go to the club that night?”

Hex answered. “Yes.”

“Did you go with him?”

“No. I got there first. She was there when I went in.”

“Were you the last person she spoke to?”

“I don't know.”

She turned because she wanted to see his face when he said that. He looked tired and frightened. She had rarely seen those two things on anyone so clearly at the same time. Last night she might have put a hand on his arm because of it. This morning she needed her hands free.

“Did you know who I was when you bought me that drink?”

“Yes.” He gave her time after that word. “There was a photograph in my instructions. I kept it.”

She went back to the window. At the bakery across the market, somebody was putting a tray of buns in the glass. She watched them slide one bun back into line after it fell over.

“I got to tell you about her without feeling like I had to explain everything. I thought it was because you were listening.”

“I was listening.”

“I know. That's what makes it worse just now.”

Nobody had an answer that would help. There was a trolley rattling down the hall. Daniel went to open the door a little so the housekeeper could pass. She said good morning like it really was just a morning, and went on with her work.

Ninja folded Elise's letter and put it in the inner pocket of her coat. She thought of all the things she could ask until she was too tired to go anywhere. She found one she needed to hear first.

“Are you in trouble if they know where you are?”

“Probably.”

“Am I?”

“I think you might be.”

She looked at him again. He was holding his hands still by gripping one wrist. That made more sense to her than the part about the photograph had. It was the first thing he'd shown her all morning that didn't feel like it had been rehearsed.

“All right. I want a cup of tea. Somewhere I can see the door. And I want you to tell me who else knows my name.”

By eight the market was filling up. They came out of the hotel separately. Ninja asked Daniel to go first because he had his hat in his hand and looked as if he might drop it if she gave him anything else to do. She waited while Hex put on one boot, then went down without him.

Ada opened the Crooked Lantern early for the people who brought her bread. She was holding a crate level with her hip when Ninja walked past the window. There was a chalk mark on her sleeve. She turned the latch and let her in, took one look at the expression on her face and poured water into the kettle.

“We can do toast as well,” she said. “I won't make you choose anything complicated.”

Ninja sat in a booth with a clear view of the door. A stack of menus leaned against the wall. She turned the top one over and found the same cheap paper on the back that her aunt used for shopping lists.

Daniel came in and put his cap on the bench. Together they waited for Hex. Ninja found she was watching the door even while she was pretending to read the menus. Last night she'd taken that habit of his as evidence that he wanted to listen the instant she spoke. She had never wondered who else he'd been waiting to see.

When he arrived, he carried a narrow paper bag from the bakery. He put it down in front of her.

“There's a bun in that. You left the hotel before I could ask.”

She left it where it was for a moment. Then she folded down the sides so she could take half. She left him the other half. It made her feel slightly better to have a thing she could share without having to tell herself what it meant.

Hex said that the evening Elise died, a man named Carver had sent him to the club to collect an envelope. When Hex got there, Elise told him she'd changed her mind about handing it over. He'd tried to get her out when Carver arrived. He had obeyed an order to move his car instead of staying with her, and was halfway down the stairs when he heard her chair scrape across the floor. He went back. By then it was too late.

Ninja didn't ask him what he had seen when he went back. She could see that he'd thought about answering it every time he came to that part of his own account. She might need it one day. For now she wanted to know what her sister had been trying to tell people.

“What did she find?”

Daniel took an old receipt out of his wallet. He drew two marks on the back. One was for a payment entered before a job. One was for money entered afterwards. Under the later figure he drew a line.

“Once I knew to look for that, there were a dozen. Sometimes the same man signed for both. Sometimes the second signature was only there because somebody had traced the first one.”

“Why would someone pay twice?”

“I don't think they did. I think the second name was put there so nobody would ask where the money actually went.”

He slid the receipt over to her. She tucked it into the same pocket as the letter. The part of the story she could follow was all written down in ordinary handwriting now. She liked that. She could read it again later when she was able to think about it without seeing the damp shoulders of a man standing outside a hotel room.

Ada replenished the tea without asking. In the mirror over the bar, Ninja watched a stranger come up to the front window and check his watch. He seemed to be waiting for somebody else. He stayed long enough to see Hex at the table before he walked on.

Hex followed him with his eyes. Ninja put her hand flat on the table.

“Tell me if you recognize him.”

“I know the car he's been driving. I saw it outside the garage yesterday. He asked Len about getting a light fixed, then made another appointment and never came back.”

Daniel turned the receipt that was no longer in front of him. He caught himself doing it and folded his hands.

“We should go somewhere else for a while,” he said. “There's a dry way along the river. I used to take it as a boy.”

“I don't want to spend this whole day being led out of rooms.”

Hex answered her quietly. “There's another way out of the village. It goes to the hidden valley. People there knew Elise. If we walk, we can talk on the way and you can turn round any time you want.”

Ninja held the cup with both hands. She'd been meaning to spend the day doing absolutely nothing. She was briefly furious with every person who had got hold of that plan without having to ask permission. Then she thought about going back up to room six and leaving Bellwether on the noon train. A day from now she would still have the letter. A week from now she would need to know what Hex meant by people who knew Elise.

“I need to get my bag first. I'll get it myself.”

He nodded. “I'll wait at the corner.”

She finished her tea. By then Ada had gone away to cut another loaf, and Daniel had got up to watch the water on the window. Ninja put the cup on the saucer without making a sound.

She took her time upstairs. The bedspread had been drawn up and the two glasses washed. She found a sock under the edge of the bed. It was her own and she put it straight in her bag. Before she left she looked at the one-eyed fish key. She could see why she'd wanted to take a picture of it. If she got to tell somebody about this morning one day, she hoped she would remember to tell them the fish was funny before any of the other things happened.

Hex was under the baker's awning with his hands in his pockets. The marzipan rabbit was back in his place. It had shifted slightly in the night, so his left paw hung over the edge of his paper base. Ninja stood beside Hex and looked at it.

“Does that rabbit actually belong to anyone?”

“I've never asked.”

“Four Tuesdays and you never asked about the biscuits. Five years and you never asked about the rabbit. I'm starting to see a pattern.”

Hex laughed once, quietly. Then he looked at her to see what was safe to make of it. Ninja put the shoulder strap of the bag in a more comfortable place.

“I still want to hear the rest. And if I feel like being angry while you tell me, that's my business.”

“Fair enough.”

She stood beside him another moment. Across the market Daniel was waiting between two shuttered stalls. There was a rise beyond him where the roofs came to an end and the rain stopped shining in the shop lights. Ninja could see a little darker patch halfway up the hill. She thought it might be trees.

They crossed the market together. Ninja got a few steps ahead because she wanted to be first out of the village. Hex let her.
