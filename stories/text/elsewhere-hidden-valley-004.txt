The service stairs were narrower than the main stairwell, the walls close and warm. They went down fast, Elias counting floors under his breath. At the bottom, a door opened onto a short corridor that ended in a swinging double door. Beyond it, the clatter of pots and the hiss of steam.

Elias pushed through first. The kitchen was busy, cooks in white coats shouting over the roar of extractor fans. A man near the delivery door looked up as they passed, his eyes narrowing. Elias didn’t slow. He walked straight to the door, shouldered it open, and pulled Ninja through into the alley beyond.

The alley was narrow, choked with crates and bins. At the far end, Bellwether market opened up, a sprawl of stalls and awnings and crowds. Elias turned toward the awnings. Ninja could see people there. She’d never been so glad to see a crowd.

The man from the landing was between two stalls, standing where he could see the mouth of the lane. Ninja caught the back of Elias’s coat. He turned just as the man looked their way.

The change in him was so quick she felt it before she understood it. He stopped breathing through his mouth. His shoulders dropped. When he spoke, he sounded as though he had all the time in the world.

“Go past the bakery,” he told her. “I’ll be right behind you.”

The man came toward them. Elias stepped into his path.

“You went inside my house,” he said.

The man gave him a little smile. “You got the message, then.”

“I got it. Next time bring it yourself. Leave the people in my house out of it.”

He said it so evenly that the woman sorting apples beside them went on sorting apples. The man’s smile disappeared. Elias waited for him to move first. When he did, Elias turned his back on him and joined Ninja at the bakery.
